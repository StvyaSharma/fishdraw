# Fish morphology for procedural side views

Researched 2026-10-28. These are **inspiration presets, not species identification drawings**. Biological anchors below come from museums, universities and fisheries institutions; proposed controls and rendering shortcuts are design recommendations. Ratios, coordinates and random variation require calibration against species references before claiming anatomical accuracy.

## Most useful drawing dimensions

Use a local axis from snout (`x=0`) to caudal-fin base (`x=1`), with body depth excluding fins. Keep tail length separate. These normalized coordinates are a proposed implementation convention.

| Dimension | Useful controls and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Body profile | Fusiform/torpedo, deep oval/discoid, elongate/pike-like, anguilliform/eel-like; independent dorsal and ventral curves, maximum depth and its position, head slope, snout length, peduncle depth. Lateral compression describes thickness, not just a tall silhouette; dorsoventral flattening is a different axis. [University of Hawaiʻi anatomy](https://www.manoa.hawaii.edu/exploringourfluidearth/biological/fish/structure-and-function-fish) |
| Tail | Rounded, truncate, emarginate/shallow notch, forked, lunate/crescent; control spread, notch depth, lobe length and asymmetry. Heterocercal tails have unequal lobes; eel fins may merge around the tail. These need different geometry, rather than one triangle stretched. [Hawaiʻi fin overview](https://www.manoa.hawaii.edu/exploringourfluidearth/biological/fish/structure-and-function-fish), [sturgeon](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/caribbean/en/thefishes/species/2760), [morays](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/Home/family/44) |
| Fin arrangement | An array of dorsal/anal/pectoral/pelvic/adipose/finlet elements, each with presence, origin, insertion, height envelope, ray count, membrane depth and spine/soft-ray rendering. Dorsal, anal and caudal are median fins; pectoral and pelvic fins are paired. [Hawaiʻi fins and rays](https://www.manoa.hawaii.edu/exploringourfluidearth/biological/fish/structure-and-function-fish) |
| Mouth/head | Separate terminal (front), superior (upward), inferior/subterminal (underside) position from gape, jaw projection, protrusion and snout length. Protrusion is not a mutually exclusive mouth position. [Florida Museum mouth examples](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/fish/anatomy/mouth-types/), [sturgeon protrusible inferior mouth](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/caribbean/en/thefishes/species/2760) |
| Lateral line | Optional path with start/end, curvature and pore spacing; keep it separate from pigment stripes. It is a sensory structure, not necessarily a prominent dark stroke. Support incomplete lines: the foureye butterflyfish has a high arch ending beneath the dorsal fin. [Hawaiʻi lateral line](https://www.manoa.hawaii.edu/exploringourfluidearth/biological/fish/structure-and-function-fish), [Smithsonian butterflyfish](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/caribbean/en/thefishes/species/3842) |
| Barbels/appendages | Arrays of anchored curves with individual lengths and visibility. Preserve attachment sites and distinguish sensory barbels from head tentacles. Catfish have chin, snout and mouth-corner barbels; sturgeon have four beneath the snout, before the mouth. [Florida Museum catfish](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/channel-catfish/), [Smithsonian sturgeon](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/caribbean/en/thefishes/species/2760) |

## Surface and markings should be independent layers

| Surface mode | Biological basis → economical line-art treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Cycloid | Overlapping scales with smooth posterior edges → staggered smooth arcs, not complete circles. |
| Ctenoid | Overlapping scales with comb-like posterior edges → similar arcs with sparse short edge teeth. At small size it is reasonable to omit the teeth. Both descriptions: [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/cycloid-and-ctenoid-scales/). |
| Ganoid | Gar have hard diamond-shaped scales → rhombic tessellation rather than scallops. [Florida Museum gar](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/florida-gar/) |
| Placoid | Shark/ray denticles with posteriorly directed structures → fine directional ticks/stipple at drawing scale, not large overlapping bony-fish scales. [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/placoid-scales/) |
| Scutes | Sturgeon have five longitudinal rows of bony plates → discrete raised shields on a few paths; these are not an all-over gar diamond mesh. [Smithsonian](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/caribbean/en/thefishes/species/2760) |
| Smooth / texture omitted | Morays and channel catfish are scaleless. Separately allow rendering scales as invisible: “no scale strokes” should not imply biological absence. [Museums Victoria/OzFishNet morays](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/Home/family/44), [Arizona Game & Fish catfish](https://www.azgfd.com/species/channel-catfish/) |

Markings need independently composable primitives: **bars** (across body depth), **longitudinal stripes** (head to tail), diagonal/chevron lines, spots, ringed **ocelli**, reticulated networks and dark-back/light-belly fields. Parameters: count, width, orientation, spacing, contrast/polarity, jitter and body/fin masks. Biological examples are lionfish bars, butterflyfish chevrons and ocellus, moray reticulation, and the cleaner wrasse's stripe widening toward the tail. Sources: [lionfish](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/common-lionfish-pterois-volitans/), [butterflyfish](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/caribbean/en/thefishes/species/3842), [morays](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/Home/family/44), [cleaner wrasse](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/common-cleanerfish-labroides-dimidiatus/). A reticulated pigment network should remain separate from scale outlines.

## Eight coherent inspiration presets

The final column gives **artistic choices**, not measured anatomical values. Unspecified traits should remain conservative rather than acquiring random decorative anatomy.

| Inspiration | Evidence-backed combination to preserve | Suggested simplification / accuracy boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bluefin tuna** (*Thunnus thynnus*) | Torpedo body narrowing strongly to a slender peduncle; two separated dorsals, short pectorals, dorsal and ventral finlets, crescent tail. Dark back/pale underside. [Florida Museum](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/bluefin-tuna/) | Prioritize taper, crescent and paired finlet trains; omit tiny surface detail. Do not substitute the greatly elongated second dorsal/anal fins associated with other tuna silhouettes. Strong body stripes are unsuitable for the adult bluefin preset; [FWC](https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/profiles/saltwater/tuna/bluefin-tuna/) describes no distinct streaks/spots. |
| **Foureye butterflyfish** (*Chaetodon capistratus*) | Deep compressed disc; small mouth on short pointed snout; spiny-to-soft dorsal, angular rear dorsal/anal edges, rounded tail; rough scales; incomplete high-arched lateral line. Eye bar, diagonal chevrons, rear-flank white-ringed black ocellus. [Smithsonian](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/caribbean/en/thefishes/species/3842) | Enlarge the ocellus for legibility, keeping it on the body beneath the rear dorsal. A single eye band plus spot is an abbreviated pattern. Juveniles have additional bars and a dorsal ocellus: do not combine juvenile and adult patterns casually. |
| **Reticulated moray** (family Muraenidae inspiration) | Very long body; dorsal, caudal and anal form a continuous fin margin; no pectoral/pelvic fins or scales. Morays include reticulated, spotted and banded forms. [Museums Victoria/OzFishNet](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/Home/family/44) | Use a smooth taper and irregular pigment-cell network. This is intentionally a family-level fantasy preset, not a claim that every moray has reticulation. A standard detached forked tail or added paired fins breaks this combination. |
| **Northern pike** (*Esox lucius*) | Long body, duckbill jaws and large teeth; single soft dorsal far back, anal also near tail; adults have light round/oval spots on dark ground. [Washington fisheries](https://wdfw.wa.gov/species-habitats/invasive/esox-lucius), [Wisconsin fisheries](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/sites/default/files/topic/Fishing/Species_northernpike.pdf) | Emphasize snout and rear fin placement; keep scale strokes subtle. Generic dark vertical tiger bars would suggest another esocid/hybrid; young pike can have light bars that break into adult spots. [Minnesota DNR](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/minnaqua/speciesprofile/northern_pike.html) |
| **Atlantic sturgeon** (*Acipenser oxyrinchus*) | Elongate nearly cylindrical body, long conical snout, inferior protrusible mouth and four preceding barbels; five scute rows; dorsal well back; deeply forked asymmetrical tail with much longer upper lobe. [Smithsonian](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/caribbean/en/thefishes/species/2760) | Show dorsal shields and near-side rows only; do not flatten all five rows onto the visible flank. Keep barbels beneath the snout rather than at lip corners. Use plain intervening skin instead of diamond scales. |
| **Red/common lionfish** (*Pterois volitans*) | Compressed body, extremely long dorsal and pectoral elements, head tentacles, red/dark bands on pale ground; cycloid scales. Soft dorsal, anal and caudal fins have spots. [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/fishes/common-lionfish-pterois-volitans/), [Florida Museum](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/red-lionfish/) | Draw a few prominent spines and a fan of rays with deeply cut membranes. Omitting rays is artistic; ray counts distinguish related species. Over-eye tentacles are not mouth barbels, and spines do not imply ctenoid scales. |
| **Channel catfish** (*Ictalurus punctatus*) | Elongate body compressed toward rear; inferior mouth; eight barbels (four chin, two snout, two corners); deeply forked tail and rounded anal margin. Smooth scaleless skin, separate adipose fin, scattered dark spots often reduced in large adults. [Florida Museum](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/channel-catfish/), [Arizona Game & Fish](https://www.azgfd.com/species/channel-catfish/) | Draw visible near-side barbels with some far-side overlap, rather than fanning all eight equally. Adipose fin should be a fleshy lobe without a ray fan. This is not a universal catfish model: armored and differently tailed catfishes exist. |
| **Florida gar** (*Lepisosteus platyrhincus*) | Long torpedo body and elongated toothy snout; small rear-set fins; diamond ganoid scales; irregular dark spots on body, head and fins; irregular tail outline. [Florida Museum](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/florida-gar/) | Prioritize rigid-looking diamond armor, rear fins and broad elongated jaws. Use a rounded irregular tail silhouette as an approximation; do not treat it as a tuna crescent. This is not the extremely slender snout of every other gar species. |

## Implementation priorities and caveats

1. **Silhouette and fin topology first:** separate dorsal segments, fin absence, connected eel margins, adipose lobes and finlet arrays buy more recognizable diversity than additional random scale patterns.
2. **Map texture names explicitly.** Existing numeric `scale_type` / `pattern_type` values in `fishdraw.ts` are renderer choices; these notes do not establish biological equivalence. Likewise, current `body_height` is not automatically measured body-depth/standard-length, and fin start/end controls need conversion before using normalized anatomical coordinates.
3. **Keep a body-relative coordinate system** for scales, marks and fin anchors so changing depth or taper does not detach the ocellus, lateral line or barbels. Clip pigment independently to body and fin regions. These are implementation recommendations.
4. **Offer coherent presets plus a free-combination mode.** Label exports “inspired by” unless proportions, counts, scale distribution and life stage have been checked against a species reference. Exaggerated fins, omitted scales, fewer rays and selected visible paired fins are deliberate artistic reductions.
5. **Use species accounts to resolve broad teaching shortcuts.** Broad scale categories do not justify covering a sturgeon in gar diamonds; a spiny fish need not have ctenoid scales (red lionfish are cycloid). Side view cannot establish cross-sectional compression. Flatfish anatomy also needs separate treatment: a flatfish's two-eyed upper side is not a generic upright side-view fish flattened vertically.
